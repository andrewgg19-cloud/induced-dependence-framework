# Glossary

This glossary defines the working vocabulary for the Induced Dependence scientific program.

## Induced Dependence

A dependency relation that is produced, amplified, revealed, or transformed by a context, constraint, observation, intervention, or shared system condition.

Induced dependence is not assumed to be an intrinsic property of isolated components. It is understood as a relation that becomes meaningful under a particular structural or operational condition.

## Intrinsic Dependence

A dependency relation that belongs to the components or variables under consideration independently of a specific external observation, intervention, or contextual transformation.

Intrinsic dependence serves as a baseline contrast for induced dependence.

## Observed Dependence

A dependency relation detected through measurement, data, or empirical observation.

Observed dependence may reflect intrinsic dependence, induced dependence, confounding, measurement artifacts, or some combination of these.

## Context

The surrounding structure, condition, environment, measurement arrangement, or operational frame in which components are related, observed, or compared.

Context can shape which dependencies are possible, visible, or relevant.

## Constraint

A restriction, rule, shared condition, conservation relation, environmental pressure, or structural limitation that narrows the possible states or behaviors of a system.

Constraints can induce dependence by making otherwise independent components jointly conditioned.

## Intervention

An action that changes a system, its environment, its measurement conditions, or its operational structure.

Interventions can reveal existing dependencies or create new dependency relations.

## Dependency Transformation

A change in the dependency structure of a system across contexts, constraints, observations, or interventions.

Dependency transformation includes the appearance, disappearance, strengthening, weakening, or reconfiguration of dependency relations.

## Baseline State

The reference condition against which induced dependence is identified.

A baseline state may represent independence, weak dependence, or a previously known dependency structure before a context, constraint, observation, or intervention is introduced.

## Inducing Condition

The specific context, constraint, observation, intervention, or system condition responsible for producing or transforming a dependency relation.

Identifying the inducing condition is central to distinguishing induced dependence from ordinary correlation.

## Dependency Signature

An observable or formal pattern that indicates the presence of a dependency relation.

Dependency signatures may appear in statistical correlations, graph structures, dynamical behavior, information flow, or comparative changes across conditions.

## Domain Translation

The process of expressing induced dependence in the vocabulary of a specific field while preserving the shared conceptual structure of the master framework.

For example, domain translation may connect induced dependence to contextuality in Bell systems, perturbation in ecology, monitoring in AI observability, or operators in mathematical foundations.

# Core Claims

This document lists the central claims that the master paper should develop, defend, qualify, or test.

## Claim 1: Dependence Can Be Induced

Dependence is not always an intrinsic relation among isolated components. It can be produced, amplified, revealed, or transformed by context, constraint, observation, intervention, or shared system conditions.

## Claim 2: Induced Dependence Is Distinct From Observed Dependence

Observed dependence is a dependency relation detected through measurement or data. Induced dependence concerns the conditions that produce or transform such a relation. An observed dependency may therefore be intrinsic, induced, artifactual, confounded, or mixed.

## Claim 3: Shared Constraints Can Produce Dependency

Variables or components that are independent under a baseline condition may become dependent when they are jointly constrained by a shared rule, resource, environment, conservation relation, or measurement structure.

## Claim 4: Observation Can Transform Dependency Structure

Observation is not always passive. In some systems, the act or structure of observation changes which dependencies become possible, visible, or relevant.

## Claim 5: Intervention Can Create or Reconfigure Dependence

Interventions can alter dependency structure rather than merely expose preexisting relations. A scientific analysis should therefore ask whether an intervention reveals dependence, induces dependence, or does both.

## Claim 6: Induced Dependence Requires a Baseline

To identify induced dependence, one must compare dependency structure across conditions. A baseline state is needed to distinguish a newly induced relation from a relation that was already present.

## Claim 7: Inducing Conditions Must Be Identified

A claim of induced dependence is incomplete unless it specifies the context, constraint, observation, intervention, or system condition responsible for the dependency transformation.

## Claim 8: Induced Dependence Is Not Equivalent to Confounding

Confounding can produce observed association, but induced dependence is broader. It includes cases where a dependency relation is structurally produced by a shared condition, not merely cases where an unobserved variable explains a misleading association.

## Claim 9: The Framework Is Cross-Domain

Induced dependence can be translated across domains, including Bell-type systems, ecological networks, AI observability, and mathematical foundations, while preserving a shared conceptual structure.

## Claim 10: Formalization Is Possible

Induced dependence can be formalized through probabilistic, graph-theoretic, information-theoretic, causal, or dynamical models. The mathematical foundations paper should determine which formalism provides the strongest base for the program.

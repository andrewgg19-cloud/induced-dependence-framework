# Ecology Paper Outline

## Working Title

Induced Dependence in Ecological Systems

## Central Thesis

Ecological dependencies can be induced or transformed by environmental constraints, perturbations, resource structure, spatial organization, and shared adaptive pressures.

## Proposed Structure

1. Introduction
   - Ecology as a domain of dynamic interdependence.
   - Why induced dependence is useful for ecological explanation.

2. Background
   - Ecological networks.
   - Competition, mutualism, predation, and indirect effects.
   - Perturbation and cascading change.

3. Induced Dependence Perspective
   - Environmental pressure as a dependency-inducing condition.
   - Shared resource constraints.
   - Dependence created by disturbance or intervention.

4. Model Cases
   - Predator-prey example.
   - Mutualistic network example.
   - Resource competition example.
   - Perturbation-driven coupling.

5. Simulation Plan
   - Network simulation with changing environmental constraint.
   - Compare baseline independence with induced coupling after perturbation.
   - Track resilience, cascade size, and dependency shifts.

6. Implications
   - Ecological resilience.
   - Conservation and intervention.
   - Interpreting observed correlations in field data.

7. Conclusion
   - Summary and connection to the broader program.

## Needed Work

- Select one primary ecological model.
- Define measurable dependency changes.
- Connect simulation outputs to manuscript claims.

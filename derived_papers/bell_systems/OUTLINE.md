# Bell Systems Paper Outline

## Working Title

Induced Dependence in Bell-Type Systems

## Central Thesis

Bell-type systems may be analyzed as cases where dependency structure is shaped by measurement context, experimental constraints, and the conditions under which correlations become observable.

## Proposed Structure

1. Introduction
   - Bell systems as a stress test for theories of dependence.
   - Why nonclassical correlations matter for induced dependence.

2. Background
   - Bell-type experimental setup.
   - Measurement settings, outcomes, and correlation constraints.
   - Locality, contextuality, and observed dependence.

3. Induced Dependence Perspective
   - Measurement context as a dependency-inducing structure.
   - Distinguishing induced dependence from hidden direct causation.
   - Relation to contextuality and constraint-based explanations.

4. Formal or Conceptual Model
   - Minimal variables and settings.
   - Candidate dependency diagrams.
   - Possible comparison with classical correlation models.

5. Implications
   - What the framework clarifies.
   - What it does not claim to solve.
   - Relationship to the master paper.

6. Conclusion
   - Summary and next steps.

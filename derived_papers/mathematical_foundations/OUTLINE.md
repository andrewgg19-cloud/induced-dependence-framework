# Mathematical Foundations Paper Outline

## Working Title

Mathematical Foundations of Induced Dependence

## Central Thesis

Induced dependence can be formalized as a transformation of dependency structure under context, constraint, observation, or intervention.

## Proposed Structure

1. Introduction
   - Need for a formal account.
   - Relationship to the master paper.

2. Preliminaries
   - Variables, systems, contexts, and observations.
   - Dependence relations.
   - Candidate mathematical languages.

3. Definitions
   - Baseline dependence.
   - Observed dependence.
   - Induced dependence.
   - Context and constraint operators.

4. Formal Properties
   - Conditions for emergence of induced dependence.
   - Persistence and reversibility.
   - Monotonicity or non-monotonicity under added constraints.
   - Distinguishing induced dependence from confounding.

5. Minimal Models
   - Probabilistic model.
   - Graph-theoretic model.
   - Dynamical systems model.
   - Information-theoretic model.

6. Candidate Propositions
   - Shared constraints can induce dependence among initially independent variables.
   - Observational partitions can create apparent dependency structure.
   - Interventions can transform the dependency graph of a system.

7. Counterexamples and Limits
   - Cases where apparent dependence is not induced dependence.
   - Cases where induced dependence is not detectable from observation alone.

8. Conclusion
   - Formal summary.
   - Open mathematical problems.

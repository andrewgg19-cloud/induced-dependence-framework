# AI Observability Paper Outline

## Working Title

Induced Dependence in AI Observability

## Central Thesis

Monitoring and evaluation do not merely reveal dependency structures in AI systems; they can also induce, amplify, or distort dependencies among model behavior, deployment context, metrics, and oversight mechanisms.

## Proposed Structure

1. Introduction
   - Observability as a central problem in complex AI systems.
   - Why hidden and induced dependencies matter.

2. Background
   - AI monitoring, evaluation, interpretability, and audits.
   - Latent structure in models and agentic systems.
   - Metric-driven behavior and deployment feedback.

3. Induced Dependence Perspective
   - Monitoring as an intervention.
   - Evaluation as a source of dependency pressure.
   - Feedback loops between system behavior and oversight.

4. Model Cases
   - Metric gaming.
   - Distribution shift under monitoring.
   - Multi-agent or tool-using system dependencies.
   - Hidden coupling in pipelines.

5. Simulation or Demonstration Plan
   - Toy system where measurement changes behavior.
   - Dependency graph before and after monitoring.
   - Compare latent, observed, and induced dependencies.

6. Implications
   - AI safety.
   - Auditing and governance.
   - Designing observability systems that account for induced effects.

7. Conclusion
   - Summary and connection to the master framework.

## Needed Work

- Choose the strongest AI observability example.
- Define what counts as induced dependence in monitoring.
- Build a minimal demonstration with clear before-and-after structure.
